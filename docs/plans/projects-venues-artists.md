*Plan, signed off by Noah and Kathryn · build in progress.*
*Draft 4, revised 5 November 2026 after the final Astra and Fable reviews. For the EI team.*

## The idea in one paragraph

Today every listing on EI stands alone. If "Ha Ha Ha" plays Chicago, New York and Zoom, those are three unrelated listings, and nothing on the site knows they are the same show. The plan adds two new kinds of page, a **Project** (the show itself, wherever it plays) and a **Venue** (a real place), and, behind the scenes for now, **Artists** (people).

Every listing we have today stays, with the same ID. We call each listing an **instance**: one run of a show by one presenter. Listings get linked to projects by people, one at a time; nothing is converted in bulk.

Flushment Day keeps ID 6275 and its page; once linked, its page says "Part of [project]".

## The four things

| | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Project** | The show itself: name, what it is about, main image, the company that made it, its writers. | Ha Ha Ha. Burnout Paradise. |
| **Instance** (today's listing) | One presenter's run: place or platforms, dates, tickets, warnings, age range, accessibility, its own cast and crew. It can have its own name. | Ha Ha Ha in Chicago. "Ha Ha Ha: Holiday Edition" in New York. |
| **Venue** | A real place people go to. Optional. | A theatre, one escape room site. |
| **Artist** | A person who makes work. | A director, writer, actor, designer. |

"Instance" is our word; visitors never see that word. Runs are listed by city or platform, dates and presenter.

**Companies stay organizers.** A project is **made by** one organizer; each instance is **presented by** an organizer, often the same one, not always: Ha Ha Ha has had a different presenter in nearly every city.

### What counts as one project

The test is **who made it**, not who presents it.

- Same show in other cities, on other platforms, back next year, with a different cast, or with its own subtitle: **same project**, new instances.
- Different presenters of the same company's show: **same project**; each sets its own warnings and age range.
- A different company staging the same script its own way: **a different project**. The same company's different show on the same material (Phantom and Masquerade): **two projects**.
- A revival or new version by the same company is **the company's call**; moderators can correct it, and can merge two projects later.

## What each one holds

**Kathryn's warning shapes this.** Each presenter can change content warnings and age range, so the same show will not always carry the same details.

- **Each instance keeps all of its own details**, exactly as listings do today. Nothing is silently pulled from somewhere else.
- **The project holds only its own page**: name, "about the show", main image, the company that made it, its writers. It holds no price; its page *shows* a "from" price, the cheapest ticket across the runs on now or coming up.
- **Credits work the IMDb and IBDB way** (Chris's decision). The show carries only its writers; every run carries its own full list of everyone else. A run that joins a show starts with a copy of the presenter's own last run of that show if they have one, otherwise of the show's most recent run, and edits it; a listing that already has credits keeps them, and linking never deletes data. Credits are grouped as writing, creative, cast and production (a game master counts as cast); the groups will change as we go, and changing them is easy. Every artist is one record with its own ID, credited on as many runs and shows as they work on, and never listed twice as two people on one run. One person can hold several roles on a run; each role is its own line under the same person. Each run also has an optional **Special Thanks** list of plain names (Kickstarter backers, friends and family) that are not artist records.
- **Starting a new instance** fills the form from the project's page plus the organizer's own previous listing of it, never another presenter's details (credits aside). Warnings, age range, accessibility, address and tickets must be confirmed before submitting.

## How a project comes to be

A show gets its project page with its second run: the project is made when a moderator approves a second instance. A show with one listing has its event page, as today.

When an organizer types a name, EI looks for shows with a similar name and asks "Is this a new run of one of these? A new city, new dates, or a remount." (Kathryn's wording). Pick one and the form starts filled in from it; pick none and it is a new listing. In the queue the moderator sees the listing asked to join "Ha Ha Ha", checks the show and who made it, and approves listing and link together, or the listing alone; there is no separate review. At that approval the project page is made from the first listing, which becomes an instance. The maker starts as the first listing's organizer; the moderator corrects it if need be (St. Ann's Warehouse presented Burnout Paradise first, but Pony Cam made it). A presenter who lists a show under their own organizer stays the presenter of that run; only the maker is corrected. If the company that made it has no organizer page on EI yet, the moderator creates a bare one for it (name only, nobody's team) so the project has a maker to point at. It is held by EI staff and is public straight away, and the company can claim it later.

**Old listings that keep getting new dates.** While a listing can still be edited, moving it to a new city or adding dates more than 90 days after its last ones makes the form ask "Want to start this as a new run?" It's a suggestion they can skip; nothing is blocked (Kathryn's note). Yes makes a duplicate, as below, and leaves the old listing alone.

**The 90-day lock.** Once a listing is 90 days past its last date its organizer can't edit it. Clicking Edit opens today's popup, which offers Duplicate; the lock stays. **Every duplicate of a published listing asks to join the show of the listing it came from**, whether or not that listing is part of a show yet, and however the copy is renamed (Kathryn's note). The organizer sees "This will join Ha Ha Ha" on the Name step and can remove it. The moderator confirms it in the normal queue; if the original was a one-off, that approval makes the project page. The popup stops suggesting a year in the name, because the show's title is all an organizer types and EI builds the address.

Linking existing listings is the same thing by hand: a moderator links the Adelaide listing to the original New York one, which makes the project, then links the rest. A listing nobody links keeps working as today.

**Taking a run off a show** is one action: it becomes a plain listing at its own address, and links to that run follow it (the show keeps its own address). A project left with one run stays; a moderator can dissolve it, and the last run takes the show's address.

## Web addresses

Each run sits under its show: show, then where, then the year it opens.

- The show's page: everythingimmersive.com/events/ha-ha-ha
- In Chicago, opening 2026: /events/ha-ha-ha/chicago-2026; on Zoom: /events/ha-ha-ha/zoom-2026; on several platforms at once: /events/ha-ha-ha/online-2026
- With its own name: /events/ha-ha-ha/chicago-2026-holiday-edition

**Organizers only type the show's title.** EI builds the rest from the city or platform and the dates; a run's own name is one optional question.

**Today's addresses.** A listing that is not part of a show keeps its address. **The first listing of a show** usually holds the plain address (/events/ha-ha-ha): when it joins, the project takes it over, the listing moves to /events/ha-ha-ha/chicago-2026, and old links to /events/ha-ha-ha land on the project page. **A later listing** (/events/ha-ha-ha-chicago) moves under the show, and its old address sends people straight to the new one. **If the plain name belongs to a different company's show**, a moderator gives the project another address, show name plus company. A mistyped address under a show goes to the show's page. A deleted plain listing's address is gone and free again, as today; a deleted run's address under a show falls back to the show's page. Venues get addresses of their own; organizer pages stay put.

## What changes on the site

**A listing page** looks the same, with one new line near the top: "Part of Ha Ha Ha. Also in New York and on Zoom." "Also in" names up to two other runs that are running or coming up; an embargoed run does not count until it is published. A known venue's name links to the venue page, and its "how to get there" notes (parking, transit) open from the listing's location block.

**The new project page** is the show's home: name, image, what it is about, the company that made it; where it was **first presented** (the earliest run EI knows of) and its **latest or current** run; a **"from" price**, the cheapest ticket across the runs on now or coming up (one per currency, none when there is no current run), instead of a range, because shows with many ticket tiers make a range meaningless (Kathryn's note); everywhere it plays, split into **Now running, Coming up and Past**, each run with its optional **run type** (workshop, world premiere, US premiere, regional premiere) and optional **festival** (for example SXSW or Venice); and a plain count of runs. Only published runs appear, and the page is public only while one of them is.

**The new venue page** is an organizer page for a place: name, picture, address and map, **how to get there**, what is on there now and before.

**Artists** get a database and the screens to add, edit, propose and merge people and credits, and nothing visitors see yet. Special Thanks is the same: entered and stored this round, not shown publicly. Noah's plan: credits for a few prolific shows first, then volunteer curators.

**Organizer pages** list the company's shows, each once: projects it made (marked **Original**), projects it presents, and listings not in any project. Pony Cam's page shows Burnout Paradise one time, as a stack of cards (three deep at most) with a count.

**Search and the map** stay as they are: each run is a result. Later, a touring show could appear once, with "also in 2 other cities".

**For organizers**: the Name step asks "Is this a new run of one of these? A new city, new dates, or a remount." when it finds a match, and on the Location step the **venue name offers suggestions as you type**, each shown with its address; picking one fills in the address, and typing an address EI already knows as a venue matches it the other way round. The venue name stays optional; changing the address later takes the venue off.

## Escape rooms and chains

The chain is an **organizer**; its room (Rise of the Resistance) is **one project**, made by that organizer; each site (Orlando, Anaheim) is **an instance** at **its own venue**, so one site's page shows all of its rooms.

## Secret locations

Some listings hide their address until booking. Rule: **a secret-location listing is never linked to a venue, never suggests one, and never appears on a venue page**, however it is edited. On a project page it shows its city only.

## Who can edit what

- **Made by, and who keeps the page.** Each project names the company that made it. Keeping the page is a job that normally belongs to that company's team; a moderator can hand it to another organizer, or correct who made the show.
- **Linking** is asked for by someone who manages the listing; a moderator or the company that keeps the project page approves it; when a moderator links a listing, its organizer and the company that keeps the page are told. A request the company leaves unanswered for **72 hours** goes to the moderators. Approving a link never publishes a draft.
- **Old listings** past the 90-day edit lock can still be linked, and a moderator can attach them to a venue. A duplicate asks to join by itself (see **The 90-day lock**); the moderator confirms it on approval.
- Other presenters edit only their own listings. Suggesting changes to the project page is left out of the first release (Chris, 2026-11-12); added if companies ask for it.
- **Venues and artists**: anyone on a team can propose one; moderators approve and merge. The show's writers are edited by whoever keeps the project page; a run's credits by that listing's team under today's rules; moderators can edit either, and curators and moderators can add cast to any show or run, including shows that never announce their casting.
- **If an organizer is folded into another**, its projects go with its listings; an organizer that still makes or keeps a project cannot be deleted until a moderator reassigns them. A project is never left without a company.
- Every link, approval, change and merge is logged with who did it.

## Mistakes, duplicates and web addresses

- **Merging** two duplicates keeps the losing record, points its old address at the winner, and logs what was linked to each, so a mistaken merge can be undone.
- **Web addresses**: today a rename breaks the old link. From now on a renamed or merged listing, organizer, project or venue redirects from its old address, which is never handed to anything else, with two exceptions: a listing's plain address passes to its own show (and comes back if the show is dissolved), and the existing admin tool that folds one organizer into another and gives the survivor the old address keeps doing that.

## Not in this round

Anything visitors see about artists; credit details for later (who went on at a given performance, which neither IMDb nor IBDB records; "original vs replacement" and "debut" flags); search and the map grouped by show; converting listings in bulk; a project page for a one-listing show; uploading a digital program to build the credits list; linking out to Laura Hall's database (its name is unclear in the transcript); an "is this your original work?" question for plain listings (next iteration); a "double-check with the ticketing site" note (wording to come); and, later, artists adding themselves: a signed-in person adds themselves to a show's or run's credits and the run's organizer approves it (Chris's direction; nothing in this round blocks it). Not wanted: Draft 3's hidden city codes, and renaming "organizer" to "producer". Noah suggested kicking the tires, then making noise at the start of the year.

- **Unlink** (any run, project keeps going): clear `project_id`, `instance_slug`, `edition`; copy the project's `writing` rows onto the event; then re-run `Event::finalSlug` for the event, which keeps its current `events.slug` only if that slug is neither the project's live slug nor a reserved path pointing at anything but this event (so the first listing, whose internal slug is the project's address, or the project's former address after a rename, always gets a fresh name-based slug); write a redirect from the old nested path to the event; delete only redirect rows that point at this event and equal its new address. Project-owned addresses are never touched.
- **Dissolve** (moderator; exactly one run left, per Chris's A3): copy the project's `writing` rows onto the run; clear its `project_id`, `instance_slug`, `edition`; set `events.slug` to the project's live slug (whichever run it is: the run takes the project's address); re-point every redirect row owned by the project (its former slugs, its old instance paths) at the run; write a redirect from the run's old nested path to it; soft-delete the project and release its slug to `deleted--{id}`. No redirect row is written for the project's live address, because the run now lives there. A later second run then re-creates the show by the normal takeover: a new `projects` row takes the run's slug, the run moves under it, no row is written, and the dissolved project row stays soft-deleted with its released slug, so nothing blocks the address. A project with no runs left is simply not public (no published run); it stays until a moderator links a run to it or merges it away.

**Routes and resolution**

- `GET /events/{slug}` (`events.show`): (1) live `Project` by slug, served only while it has at least one published run (otherwise 404, like an unpublished event); (2) `slug_redirects` `events/{slug}` → 301; (3) `Event` by `events.slug`: linked → 301 to `publicPath()`; else published only, as today; (4) 404. Because every slug write removes any alias of the record that equals its new live address (above), no redirect can point at itself.
- `GET /events/{project}/{instance}` (`events.instance`): (1) `Event` by `project_id` + `instance_slug`, published → show; status `e`, `r`, `n`, draft → 404 (a run taken down but not deleted shows an error page, as today); (2) `slug_redirects` for the full path, or for `events/{project}` after a project rename (301 to the new project slug plus the same instance), so a moved, unlinked or merged run's old address goes where the run is now; (3) project exists → 302 to `/events/{project}` (typos, and a deleted run's released address); (4) 404.
- `GET /organizers/{organizer}` uses implicit binding today (a renamed organizer's old slug 404s before the controller): add a `->missing()` handler that consults `slug_redirects`. `GET /venues/{venue}` as above.
- Hosting, outside `can:manage,event` (`EventPolicy::manage` is the presenter's team or a moderator, not the maker's team): `/hosting/projects/{project}/edit` and `POST /hosting/projects/{project}` (`ProjectPolicy::edit`: the page-keeping organizer's team via `User::belongsToOrganization`, or a moderator; edits the show's writers too and shows the aggregate of its runs' credits), `/hosting/projects/{project}/link-requests` with `…/{request}/approve|reject` (`ProjectPolicy::decideLink`), `DELETE /hosting/projects/{project}/instances/{event}` (unlink). Inside `can:manage,event`: `POST|DELETE /hosting/event/{event}/link-request` (`EventPolicy::requestLink`, refusing held drafts), `POST|DELETE /hosting/event/{event}/link-request` also lets a draft's team remove the request a duplicate created.
